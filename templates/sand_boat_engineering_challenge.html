🏜️ Sand Boat Engineering Challenge

The roads in our desert community have turned to deep sand. You still want to get to school to keep learning. Build a sand boat that can glide across the desert using wind power.

Back to Day 15 Lesson Back to Lab Library

Mission Scenario

Your team must design a sand boat. It needs a body shell, sail, and rudder. Each part must be made from a material with helpful physical properties.

Think like an engineer: choose materials based on state of matter, texture, mass, flexibility, strength, and ability to catch wind.

1. Choose Body Shell

The body must be solid, sturdy, and able to glide on sand.

2. Choose Sail

The sail should be light and catch the wind.

3. Choose Rudder

The rudder helps steer and should be stiff enough to guide the boat.

Science Notebook: CER Engineering Reflection

Claim: Did your sand boat reach school? Why or why not?

Evidence: Which materials did you choose? What physical properties helped or hurt the design?

Reasoning: Explain how physical properties affected the boat’s performance.

Teacher / Student Discussion Questions

1. Why would a rock body shell be a poor engineering choice for a sand boat?

2. Why might hard plastic or wood work better than a plastic bag?

3. Which physical property mattered most for the sail?

4. How is this similar to choosing materials for real objects?
