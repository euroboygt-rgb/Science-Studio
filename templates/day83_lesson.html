🔬 Tiny Particles Can Help

In the atmosphere, tiny particles such as dust, sea salt, or smoke can provide surfaces where water can condense.

These are called condensation nuclei.
🚨 Major Misconception

The white part of a cloud is NOT invisible water vapor.

A visible cloud contains huge numbers of tiny liquid droplets and/or ice crystals.

🌦️ Weather Connection

Clouds are evidence that water from the atmosphere has condensed.

Some clouds may later produce precipitation, but not every cloud produces precipitation.

📓 Science Notebook — How a Cloud Forms

Draw four connected boxes:
  1. Evaporation: liquid water becomes invisible water vapor.
  2. Cooling: moist air loses thermal energy.
  3. Condensation: gas becomes tiny liquid droplets.
  4. Cloud: many tiny droplets and/or ice crystals become visible together.
Write underneath:

Water vapor is invisible. Clouds contain tiny droplets and/or ice crystals.

🤝 Guided Practice — Cold Surface Condensation

Use a cold cup, bottle, or container.
  1. Observe the outside before condensation appears.
  2. Wait for droplets to form on the outside.
  3. Ask: Did the water leak through the container?
  4. Identify water vapor in the surrounding air as the source.
  5. Connect: cooling → condensation → visible droplets.
Teacher emphasis: This models condensation, not the entire process of cloud formation.
