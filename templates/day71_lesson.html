🎯 Learning Target

We will investigate how reflection and absorption help us see the colors of objects.

🔔 Bell Ringer — Where Did the Other Colors Go?

Yesterday we learned that white light contains:

Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, and Violet.

White light shines on an orange shirt.
  1. Which visible colors strike the shirt?
  2. Which color reaches your eyes most strongly?
  3. What might happen to the other colors?

👨‍🏫 Mini Lesson — How We See Color

✨ White Light ROYGBIV travels toward the object.
→
👕 Orange Shirt Light interacts with the material.
→
🟧 Orange Reflected Orange light travels toward the eye.
→
👁️ Eye The observer sees orange.
↩️ Reflection

The color we see is light reflected from the object toward our eyes.

⬇️ Absorption

Other visible light can be taken in by the material.

⬜ White Objects

White objects reflect much of the visible light that strikes them.

⬛ Black Objects

Black objects absorb most visible light and reflect very little.

Science Rule:

The color we see depends on the light REFLECTED from the object and sent toward our eyes.

Much of the remaining visible light may be ABSORBED.

📓 Science Notebook — The Orange Shirt Model

Draw:
  1. a white-light source,
  2. ROYGBIV traveling toward an orange shirt,
  3. orange light reflecting toward an eye,
  4. the other visible colors being mostly absorbed.
Write underneath:

“The shirt appears orange because orange light is reflected toward my eyes.”
